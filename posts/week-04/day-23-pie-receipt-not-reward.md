the thing that makes liquid staking risky isn't the staking. it's that the receipt becomes a second asset with its own price.

stETH trades below ETH when people want out. that gap is the risk: you staked one thing, you now hold another, and the thing you hold has a market that can move against you independently of the asset underneath it.

@RetiumChain's $Pie works differently. it is minted 1:1 when you stake RTM and burned when you unstake. there is no price to track, no peg to defend, no discount to panic about. it's a receipt for a position, not a tradable claim on one.

that matters more than it sounds:

→ your staked position doesn't quietly become a second exposure
→ there is no liquid staking derivative that can de-peg under stress
→ unstaking is a burn, not a sale — you are not dependent on a market to exit

and the yield behind it isn't inflated into existence. RTM has a fixed supply of 1 billion with 0% inflation, and validators are paid from real transaction fees. there is no emission schedule propping up the number.

a staking receipt that cannot de-peg, sitting on top of a yield that isn't printed. that's a meaningfully different risk profile from most of what's out there.

public testnet is live if you want to look at the model yourself.
