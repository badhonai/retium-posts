```text
I've been digging into Retium's token distribution model and it's the most anti-crypto crypto launch I've seen.

here's what most L1 launches look like:

→ team takes 15-20%
→ VCs get tokens at $0.01
→ advisors get 3-5%
→ public buys at $1.00 on launch day
→ VCs unlock at month 6 and sell into retail liquidity

Retium:

→ team allocation: 0
→ investor allocation: 0
→ advisor allocation: 0
→ partner allocation: 0

nobody gets RTM before the public. nobody pays less.

instead, there's the Safehouse. an on-chain reserve that holds the supply and exchanges it at 1:1 with rUSD. same rate for everyone, always, in both directions.

deposit 1 rUSD → get 1 RTM
return 1 RTM → get 1 rUSD

the only exception is a small reserve for founding nodes, and it's locked. can never be sold.

pair that with:

• fixed supply of 1 billion RTM (no minting, ever — no admin key, no governance vote, no emergency function can change it)
• 0% inflation (validators are paid from transaction fees, not new tokens)
• 1% of every fee is permanently burned (deflationary pressure from real usage)
• 18 decimal precision

the result is a monetary model where:

𝟭. supply only decreases over time (burns, no minting)
𝟐. value enters through real usage, not speculative unlocks
𝟯. there's no hidden supply waiting to dump on you
𝟰. the token you hold today represents the same share of supply it will tomorrow (minus the burn, which makes your share slightly larger)

as a developer, this is the kind of foundation I want to build on. I don't want my dApp's economics tied to a token that has a VC cliff coming in 6 months.

the Safehouse is how RTM enters circulation when mainnet launches. same price for everyone. no insider advantage.

@RetiumChain
```
